The Brand Narrative

Not just coffee.
A point of view.

Borne isn't a café that happened to have great coffee — it's a statement. Born from Davao's thriving creative underground, Borne marks the moment specialty culture graduated from reserved to revelatory. Every cup is a commitment: to traceable origins, to precise craft, and to the belief that premium doesn't have to whisper.

Where others play it safe, Borne leans in. Our matcha is ceremonial-grade and electric-green. Our espresso pulls are dialed with obsessive intent. The space is bold, the menu is curated, and the energy — unmistakably alive. This is specialty coffee for a city finally ready to be seen.

Typography

Fraunces

Role: Display / Headlines
Style: Optical-size serif with a soft, editorial character. Italic variants carry warmth and wit.
Use: Campaign headlines, menu names, hero titles, print collateral.
Why Borne: Authoritative without being stiff — it has personality baked into every curve.

Syne Regular

Role: Body / UI / Labels
Style: Geometric sans-serif with unusual character spacing and high legibility at small sizes.
Use: Body copy, navigation, social captions, packaging labels.
Why Borne: Modern, a little raw, and distinct enough to feel designed — not defaulted to.

Design Logic

Why these choices work.

The Dark Canvas

Deep greens and near-blacks create a sense of depth and quiet confidence. The palette references earth — moss, bark, soil — grounding the brand in its natural coffee and matcha origins without feeling rustic or dated.

Electric Matcha Accent

#C4EC52 is both a literal matcha reference and a cultural signal. Acid greens are the dominant accent of cutting-edge fashion and streetwear brands globally — using it positions Borne inside contemporary culture, not behind it.

Fraunces + Syne

The pairing creates intentional tension: the warmth of a humanist serif against the crisp angularity of a geometric sans. Tension = interest. It's the typographic equivalent of artisanal craft meeting modernist design.

Premium Without Pretense

The system avoids gold, marble, and muted pastels — the tired language of "luxury coffee." Instead it uses restraint in layout, negative space, and typographic hierarchy to communicate premium through confidence, not décor.
